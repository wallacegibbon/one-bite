# 01 · Worldview and Story

[中文: ../cn/01-世界观与故事.md](../cn/01-世界观与故事.md)

## 1. Frutaria (果灵谷)

A basin completely enclosed by the **Grey Veil**, about nine kilometres across. Inside the basin there is forest, one very deep lake, a northern ridge, a ruined orchard, and a chain of **rock islands floating in the air**.

There is only one physical fact about the valley worth remembering: **every plant here tends to bear fruit, and every fruit tends to remember an animal.**

One plant never bears two kinds of fruit. If a tree bears Owlberries, it means owls have eaten from it, buried it and forgotten it repeatedly over a century. Fruit is the plant's memory, not the plant's organ.

### The Grey Veil

A grey band of fog ringing the valley, about 400 metres thick and with no visible top.

- It does **not** stop you from leaving. You can walk into it, you can come out the other side, and the far side is an ordinary hillside.
- What it stops is **coming back**. Anyone who leaves forgets within two hours why they meant to return; fruit carried out turns into ordinary fruit within a day (losing all magic).
- The only thing in the fog that fruits is the **Veilwalker Ash-berry** — it is not a plant, it is a pit grown out of some animal's dying wish.
- Veilbeasts live in the fog. They do not bite, they *take*: a backpack slot they touch goes empty, and you cannot recall what was in it.

> Design use: the world border is not a wall, it is **forgetting**. Players never hit a wall, they simply turn back — because further on there is no reason left.

## 2. Shape-eating: why there are no wands

Witches outside the valley use wands. A wand works on the principle of **pointing your finger somewhere and sending power there** — it is a directional, external tool.

Frutaria has no such thing. The reason is the **Mother Bloom**.

### The Mother Bloom (母树)

The half-dead giant tree at the centre of the Old Orchard. Every fruit tree is an extension of its root system; every fruit is a fragment of its memory. The Mother Bloom's roots run under the whole valley, and it did one thing a wand can never do:

**It planted the memory of animals inside plants.**

So magic in the valley is **inward**: you cannot push power out, you can only take something else in. Pointing, waving and chanting mean nothing here — in fact, people have historically carried wands into the valley, and inside the wand keeps *pointing* toward the Mother Bloom like a compass that is always wrong, until it cracks on its own.

> The motto of Starfall Keep: **"We do not use wands here — we do not even have the gesture of pointing."**

### The cost: the magic stomach

A human stomach cannot process two magics at once. Eating two fruits back to back causes a **churn**: vitality drops, the time bar resets, the current form is overwritten. Experienced foragers "empty out" — wait for the form to end, let the stomach go clean, then eat the next one.

(The prototype implements "overwrite + churn warning"; the full magic-stomach rules are in 04.)

## 3. The Mother Bloom's amnesia (the main-line mystery)

The Mother Bloom is forgetting.

Each time it forgets, one whole class of fruit in the valley **stops fruiting** — the tree is still alive, the fruit still has the same shape, but eating it does nothing. The player meets this in the first act: a few Owlberry trees in the Whisperwood bear *empty* fruit.

The cause of the amnesia (revealed in act three):

**The Grey Veil is the Mother Bloom.**

Some centuries ago, in order not to be cut down, the Mother Bloom grew part of itself into the fog that rings the valley. The fog is its body; the valley is its fruit. It began to forget because **it remembers too much, and nothing carried out ever comes back**.

So the real choice of the main line is:

| Ending route | What you do | The price |
|---|---|---|
| **Restitution** | Plant the Mother Bloom bud back in the Old Orchard so every memory taken away flows back | Every magic fruit in the valley permanently loses half its potency; the player can never become a dragon again |
| **Grafting** | Graft the Embercore (dragon) onto the Mother Bloom bud, growing a new memory tree | The Mother Bloom dies outright; the valley's magic moves to the Cloudspire isles, so from then on every rare fruit is in the sky and must be harvested by flight |
| **Non-intervention** | Do nothing | The valley grows "ordinary" year by year; the codex fills with more and more empty fruit |

## 4. Timeline

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| ~900 years ago | Earliest record of the Mother Bloom. Valley dwellers discover that eating fruit transforms you |
| ~600 years ago | **The first Grey Veil.** The Mother Bloom grows into the fog wall and encloses the valley. Outsiders say the place "disappeared" |
| ~400 years ago | Starfall Keep is founded. It is not a magic academy, it is a **fruit cellar and a school of identification** |
| ~300 years ago | Lanternrow forms. Its first town rule: fruit does not enter the house, only the cellar |
| ~300 years ago | The Old Orchard is abandoned. The Mother Bloom's first mass amnesia: 40 kinds of fruit go empty overnight |
| ~120 years ago | The Greyglove Trading Company is founded. Systematic export of fruit begins — the first "fruit drain" |
| 30 years ago | **The Ridge Winter.** Ash bears vanish for a year, because every cellar they buried went dormant |
| This year | The game begins. Linnaeus is 16 and enters the valley for the first time |

## 5. The seven main chapters (details in 06)

1. **Empty Fruit** — the owls' fruit in the Whisperwood is empty. Why?
2. **Identification** — Starfall Keep's greenhouse: learn to read rarity and dormancy.
3. **The Lake's Ledger** — the merrow demand that one thing in the lake be returned.
4. **The Shape of the Grove** — unicorns only attack "people pretending to be unicorns".
5. **Greyglove** — the company is shipping fruit out through the Grey Veil.
6. **Cloudspire** — the ash dragon's nest, the Embercore.
7. **The Mother Bloom** — three endings.

## 6. Tone and style

- The world does not explain itself. All setting is delivered through **fruit descriptions**, **codex entries**, **town rules** and **quest-failure messages**.
- How codex entries are written: first one verifiable fact, then one unverifiable rumour.
  > Example: "A small grey-violet berry that grows in old oak hollows; at night only you can see it faintly reflecting light. The night watch says: 'The best way to pick an Owlberry is to become an owl first.'"
- Banned words: mana, level, experience points, quest reward, upgrade (as a stat).
- The humour is dry and administrative:
  > "At most 3 cellar trees — the valley cannot remember more places than that."
