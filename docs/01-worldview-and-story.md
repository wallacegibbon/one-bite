# 01 · The World

## 1. The Valley

A basin completely ringed by **grey fog**, about nine kilometres across. Inside it: forest, one very deep lake, a north ridge, a ruined orchard, and a chain of **rock islands floating in the air**.

There are only three kinds of thing in this world:

| | |
|---|---|
| **People** | There is a town, orchards, storage. People do ordinary things: they pick, store and trade |
| **Animals** | Each species lives on its own ground, with its own abilities and its own fragility |
| **Magic fruit** | Each animal fruits on its own ground. One plant never fruits two kinds |

**The only magic: eat the fruit and you become the animal the fruit remembers; after a while you change back.**

Nothing else. No spells, no gestures, no foci, no classes, no levels, no gods, no prophecies.

> Design purpose: the less the setting explains, the more of the player's attention stays on the three things that actually matter — **body, terrain, time**.

## 2. What the player is here to do: survive, then experience

There is no mystery to solve, no one to rescue, no villain. You are here for two things.

| Survive | Experience |
|---|---|
| Fruit spoils, the backpack has 24 slots, and the valley is bigger than the life of a single fruit — so you arrange, you store, you choose | Sink to the bottom of Mirrorlake as a frog, force a path open as a bear: every body shows you a part of the valley you could not otherwise see |

Failing is simple too: drowning, the cold on the northern ridge, a frog body drying out on the shore, the fog at the edge, and time running out. There is no failure cutscene — you wake up in town, and what you were carrying lies where you fell.

## 3. The Grey Fog (the edge of the world)

A grey band of fog around the valley, roughly 400 m thick and too tall to see the top of.

- It does **not** stop you leaving. You can walk in and through it; on the other side is an ordinary hillside.
- What it stops is **coming back**. Anyone who leaves forgets, within two hours, why they were going back; fruit carried out turns into ordinary fruit within a day and loses all magic.
- It is cold inside, and the deeper you go the harder walking gets. **There are no animals in the fog** — not one living thing, which is the most unsettling part of it.

> Design purpose: the world border is not a wall, it is **forgetting**. The player never hits anything — they turn around, because further on there is no reason.

## 4. How transformation works (see 04)

- Eat the fruit → become the animal it remembers → the time bar runs out → you are human again.
- **You inherit the whole body**: everything it can do, and everything it cannot. Claws cannot open storage, antlers will not fit a low hole, skin dries out of water. **The mind is untouched** — inside a bear's body you are still you (04 §5.0).
- **One body at a time.** Eating a second fruit while you are still in a body simply changes you into that one — the body you were in ends immediately. There is no penalty and no organ that holds two magics; the cost is the body you ended early and the fruit you spent (04 §5.1).
- A fruit starts spoiling the moment it leaves the tree. A dormant fruit becomes a pit; a pit can be planted, and in a hundred years or so there may be a new fruit tree where it was.

## 5. Voice and style

- The world does not explain itself. Every piece of setting arrives through **fruit descriptions**, **journal entries**, **town rules** and **quest-failure text**.
- How journal text is written: one verifiable fact, then one unverifiable rumour.
  > e.g. "A small green fruit on a stem, in the reeds of Mirrorlake, only visible from the water. The night watch says: the best way to pick a frog pear is to become a frog first."
- Banned words: mana, level, XP, quest reward, upgrade.
- The humour is dry and administrative:
  > "Cellar trees: 3 at most — the valley cannot remember more places than that."
